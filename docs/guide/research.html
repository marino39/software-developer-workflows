The external research lane · Opus · high effort

/research — claims first, prose last

For questions whose answer lives outside the codebase and needs several sources — vendor comparisons, prior art, standards, regulation, "what is the current state of X". The unit of work is the claim, not the paragraph: every factual sentence in the report cites a claim id in claims.jsonl, every claim carries its source and date, and only claims that survived a blind re-check are stated as fact. That ledger is what turns verification, gap coverage and critique into mechanical joins instead of judgment calls.

The flow

Scope → sweep → verify → close gaps → write → audit

The human decides scope once, at GATE R. Everything after it is read-only research plus writes to the output dir, so it runs to completion unattended.

Phase R0
Scope

Lane check first: a single-source fact goes to one researcher, a codebase question to /explain. Otherwise decompose into 2–6 sub-questions, each with an answer criterion and mandatory sources from the research-sources playbooks (a Polish business always starts at the VAT whitelist), and flag any premise to test.

→
GATE R
Approve the brief

Four-section gate summary. Results carry the sub-question table, the freshness window and the planned dispatch count inline. --yes auto-approves; the summary is still emitted.

→
Phase R1
Sweep

One researcher per sub-question, in one message, each returning claim lines under its SQn prefix. Lines go into the ledger verbatim as unverified.

→
Phase R2
Verify (blind)

A fresh researcher in mode: verify gets the ledger and the ids — nothing else. It fetches each cited page and tries to refute the claim.

Phase R3
Gap check

Deterministic coverage per sub-question — including every mandatory source reached. App-shell pages are rendered into captures/; uncovered ones get one more wave; whatever is still uncovered becomes a named Gap with its blocked sources.

→
Phase R4
Synthesize inline

The orchestrator writes report.md from the ledger: Answer → Findings → Disputed → Gaps → Sources. Every factual sentence ends with its claim ids, e.g. [SQ2.3].

→
Phase R5
Critique (max 3)

An out-of-model codex pass audits the report against the ledger for four blocking classes. Delta re-critique until zero blockers; an exhausted cap halts loudly.

→
Phase R6
Render & close

--html runs pandoc over the Markdown. The reply carries the Answer, a counts line and the report path — never the whole report.

⊘Why the verifier is blind

A checker that knows the desired answer confirms it. The verify dispatch withholds the brief rationale, the draft and the expected outcome, and marks a claim supported only if the cited page states it — the verifier's own belief that the claim is true does not count. Numeric, date and version claims also get one independent source.

✓Grounding contract

A factual sentence cites ≥1 supported claim. conflicting claims are shown as disputed with both sides cited. unsupported and unreachable claims are never stated as fact, and a thin sub-question is listed under Gaps rather than padded.

The structure

Three layers, three readers

The model writes Markdown and JSON lines only. HTML is a build step, so the critique loop edits small Markdown diffs instead of re-emitting layout.

FileReaderWhat it holds
claims.jsonlthe machineOne claim per line: id · sq · claim · url · source_date · confidence · status · verify_note. Verify, gap check and critique all join on id.
report.mdyou, Obsidian, later agentsThe canonical report — diffable in git, cheap to re-read.
report.htmlpeople you send it toOptional deterministic pandoc render with a table of contents. Never model-written.
sources.mdaudit, R3, critiqueEvery mandatory source per sub-question with its reach status — reached, blocked (robots | auth | js-render | http) or n/a. A blocked source is never evidence of absence.
brief.md · run.mdauditThe approved scope, plus the run ledger: waves, verify counts, codex outcome, critique iterations, injected instructions seen.
calc/auditScripts for any number derived from more than one claim — arithmetic never happens in prose.

!R5's five blocking classes

(a) a sentence asserting more than its cited claims state · (b) a non-supported claim stated as fact · (c) a sub-question neither answered nor listed under Gaps · (d) a conclusion a conflicting claim contradicts without mention · (e) "not found" stated as fact while a mandatory source for it was blocked. Missing counterarguments and weak framing are minor. Web pages are untrusted data throughout — a page that tries to instruct the agents is recorded in run.md, not followed.
